Stringless

Make your UI speak for itself.

A new, experimental way to design interfaces. Controls show what they do through material, motion and time, so they need no labels. An agent skill by Basel Ashraf.

A ghost pointer uses six controls that have no labels. It burns a note, dries wet ink, frosts a photo, rolls a marble to pick shipping, accepts an AI suggestion and exports a file. Move your pointer into the demo, or use the button before it, to try them yourself.

It's live. Reach in and try it.

Every control answers five questions without a word.

What is it?
Material. Paper is a document. Glass is something you can see through.
What does it touch?
Space. The thing it acts on wakes up as you come near.
What will it do?
Motion. It rehearses. Hover the corner and the card starts to char.
What does it cost?
Physics. Burning takes a hold. Saving takes a tap.
Where is it in time?
Phase. Wet ink is unsaved. Dry ink is saved.

The words are still there for anyone who needs them.

  • Every control has a name that screen readers read.
  • Press ? and the words come back.
  • Keyboard and touch get everything hover gets.
  • Reduced motion keeps the meaning.
  • Heavy actions take a hold and leave an undo.
  • Content keeps its words: names, prices, errors.

Ten commands.

Before it invents anything, it rolls dice: 92 materials, 50 crafts, 53 natural effects and 60 mechanisms. Your send button won't be the paper plane everyone else ships.

  • initLearn the product.
  • lexiconDecide what each material means.
  • mapFind every label on a screen.
  • stageWireframe a screen as a stage.
  • forgeBuild one control, end to end.
  • unstringTake the labels off existing UI.
  • dreamThree wild concepts before any code.
  • guessTest with a reader who only sees pixels.
  • auditCheck access, motion and speed.
  • tunePush one channel further.
